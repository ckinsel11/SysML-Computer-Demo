Change Management. A concept that seems so simple and helpful, but is the bane of many's existence. 

When should you implement a Change Management process?
    Change Management should be implemented after you establish the baseline for your model. This shouldn't be extremely early in the development,
    as you will probably slow yourself down too much with added process. 

How do you go about change management for a SysML model?
    1. Plan. You don't have to be agile necessarily, but have a release plan of "versions" of the model.
        I  like using semantic versioning for my releases of a product. 
            Establish rules for what is a major/minor/patch release. 
        Plan out what you want the next version(s) to look like and write tasks to achieve those goals. 

    2. Execute on those tasks.
        Work to the plan and burn down the tasks for the next release.

    3. Verify completion of tasks
        Have another person verify the results of that task, so that it is independently verified.
    
    4. Generate Artifacts
        The model itself is an artifact, but there are also other documents (ICDs, Specs, etc.) that can be generated from the model.
        All of these need to be generated. 
    
    5. Review Artifacts
        Diffs can easily be generated for those document-based artifacts. 
            That will be the real test if the changes are accurate because those artifacts tend to be other team's touch points to the model.
        For the model, you can do a diff of all of the content, however SysML v1 does not make this easy, but in V2 the textual notation can be diffed.
            This text diff will give every change imaginable other than pixel shifts on diagrams, and may not be the most helpful out of context.
    
    6. Approve the changes. 

    7. Release the products and artifacts.
        Snap your baseline and generate the final artifacts from that approved baseline.
