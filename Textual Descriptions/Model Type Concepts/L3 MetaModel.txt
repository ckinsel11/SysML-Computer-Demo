Layer 3 - Metamodel

What are metamodels?
    Metamodels are usually pictoral views of a generic architecture that describe how all elements are to be used and connected in that architecture. 

Why are metamodels important?
    Metamodels visually help the end users understand whether what they're doing is "right" or "wrong".
    They can also form the foundation for establishing validation rules. 

Who needs metamodels?
    These are used by the end users early on to understand how to build the model.
    When there are complete constructs and work instructions, metamodels can be almost pushed to irrelevant.

Who should be making metamodels?
    There should be one for the profiles created and maintained by the methodology team. Projects may have their own for unique data and element types.

When do you define metamodels?
    These are done after the profiles are created and before getting too deep into an architecture so that it can help enforce consistency.
    Consistency is key for modeling and it's best to get that established as early as possible.

Where do you define a metamodel?
    Metamodels can be cross cutting. 
    There should be one on in the profile that demonstrates the usages of the constructs/stereotypes.
    Each project can have their own that extends the metamodel in the profile for anything that is domain specific.

How do metamodels get updated?
    Metamodels don't change very much after the modeling decisions get made. 
    However, these  will get updated when something about the model gets matured. (e.g. Going from a "has a" to a "is a" relationship)
